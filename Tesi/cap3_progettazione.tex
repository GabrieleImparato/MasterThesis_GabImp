
\section{Motivation}

The assignment of warehouse missions to resources is formulated as a discrete
receding-horizon optimization problem. At each decision instant, the current
workload of the resources is observed and the feasible assignments are
obtained from the colored Petri net. The MPC evaluates the predicted workload
for each feasible assignment and applies only the best current decision. The
procedure is then repeated after the state of the system has been updated.

This formulation differs from classical linear MPC, in which the manipulated
variables are usually continuous. In the present application, an assignment
is binary: a mission is either assigned to a resource or it is not. Therefore,
the CPN is responsible for the discrete feasibility constraints, while the
MPC cost function ranks the feasible alternatives.

\section{Workload Model}

Let $x(k) \in \mathbb{R}^{n_r}$ denote the accumulated workload of the
$n_r$ resources at decision step $k$. The workload of resource $r$ is the
estimated time required to complete the missions already assigned to it. The
assignment vector is denoted by $u(k)$. Its elements are binary variables,
with
\begin{equation}
	u_i(k) \in \{0,1\}.
\end{equation}

For the current mission, the duration associated with each feasible resource
is calculated by the A* path-planning module. These durations form the matrix
$B$. The workload model used by the scheduling MPC is
\begin{equation}
	x(k+1) = A x(k) + B u(k),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
	A = I_{n_r}.
\end{equation}

The identity matrix expresses the fact that the workload is accumulated: the
previous workload is retained and the duration of the newly assigned mission
is added to the corresponding resource.

\section{Prediction Matrices}

For a prediction horizon $N$, the workload predictions are stacked in the
vector $Y(k)$. The prediction model can be written as
\begin{equation}
	Y(k) = P x(k) + H U(k),
\end{equation}
where $U(k)$ contains the future binary assignments and $P$ and $H$ are the
free-response and forced-response prediction matrices, respectively.

In the implementation, these matrices are generated by
\texttt{mpcPredictionMatrices.m}. The current implementation uses a horizon
of three decision steps. At each invocation of the selector, candidate
assignments are generated only among the resources allowed by the color
matrix. The first assignment is then applied to the Petri net and the
prediction is recomputed at the next decision instant.

\section{Normalized Cost Function}

The assignment is selected by minimizing a normalized multi-objective cost.
Let $T_r^{\mathrm{pred}}$ be the final predicted workload of resource $r$ and
let $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$ be the reference workload, set to eight hours. The
three normalized terms are
\begin{align}
	J_{\mathrm{max}} & =
	\frac{\max_r T_r^{\mathrm{pred}}}{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}, \\
	J_{\mathrm{std}} & =
	\frac{\operatorname{std}(T^{\mathrm{pred}})}{T_{\mathrm{ref}}}, \\
	J_{\mathrm{var}} & =
	\frac{\sum_r \left(T_r^{\mathrm{pred}} - \overline{T}^{\mathrm{pred}}\right)^2}
	{T_{\mathrm{ref}}^2},
\end{align}
where $\overline{T}^{\mathrm{pred}}$ is the mean predicted workload.

The complete objective function is
\begin{equation}
	J = w_1 J_{\mathrm{max}} + w_2 J_{\mathrm{std}} + w_3 J_{\mathrm{var}},
\end{equation}
with the weights
\begin{equation}
	w_1 = 0.60, \qquad w_2 = 0.25, \qquad w_3 = 0.15.
\end{equation}

The first term gives priority to the total completion time. The second and
third terms penalize an uneven distribution of work among the resources. The
normalization by $T_{\mathrm{ref}}$ makes the terms comparable and avoids a
single term dominating only because of its numerical scale.

\section{Receding-Horizon Implementation}

The implemented scheduling loop follows these steps:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item The CPN identifies the transitions that can be fired and the
	resources compatible with the current mission.
	\item A* calculates the predicted mission duration for every compatible
	resource.
	\item The matrices $P$ and $H$ predict the resulting workload for each
	candidate assignment.
	\item The normalized cost $J$ is evaluated for every candidate.
	\item The candidate with minimum cost is selected and converted into the
	color vector required by the CPN.
	\item The CPN executes the selected transition and updates the marking,
	resource position, and accumulated workload.
\end{enumerate}

Only the current assignment is applied. The optimization is repeated after
this update, which gives the method its receding-horizon character.

\section{Completion-Time Criterion}

The cost function is used for local assignment decisions, whereas the total
simulation time is evaluated separately. A solution is considered acceptable
when the final makespan does not exceed $8.5$ hours. A makespan between $7.5$
and $8.5$ hours is reported as being inside the target window, while a result
below $7.5$ hours is still accepted as a faster-than-target solution. This
separation prevents the global completion-time requirement from distorting
the local assignment cost.
